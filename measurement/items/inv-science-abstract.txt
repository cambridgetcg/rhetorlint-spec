Background: Soil moisture influences the germination rate of many annual plants, but field estimates for Arabidopsis thaliana remain sparse. Methods: We sowed 2,400 seeds across 48 plots at three sites in southern Sweden and recorded emergence daily for 30 days. Soil water content was measured at 5 cm depth with capacitance probes. Results: Germination increased from 31% (95% CI 27–35) in the driest quartile to 58% (95% CI 54–62) in the wettest. The effect was smaller at the northern site, where mean temperatures were 2.1 °C lower. Seeds that were buried by rainfall-driven sediment were excluded from the analysis. Conclusions: Within the range we observed, higher soil moisture was associated with higher germination. Because plots were not randomized within sites, these results may be confounded by microsite differences, and we cannot exclude an effect of seed age.
